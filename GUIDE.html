What I Know of the Well

Being the collected notes of a delver of long residence: still alive, still descending, still looking for the way out. Left where you would find them.

To Whoever Finds This

I don't know how long I've been down here. I stopped scratching tally marks when the wall ran out. What I have instead of a count of days is a count of everything else: every beast, every bottle, every lying door. I write it down. Mock me for it if you like; the counting is why I'm still breathing and better delvers than me are not.

I have not found the way out. I've found the next best thing: all the ways not to die. They're yours now.

Read everything. The Well does not forgive an uneducated guess, and some of what's written here (the answers to the doors especially) was paid for in blood that wasn't always mine. If you'd rather earn your own scars, stop reading at the section I've marked. I won't think less of you. I won't think of you at all, most likely, unless you wedge the way out open behind you.

The Shape of the Place

They call it the Well. Nobody dug it. It goes down forever, or at least farther than anyone has lived to disprove.

Each floor is rooms and corridors, never the same twice. I've paced them out: never fewer than eight rooms, rarely more than fourteen. Somewhere on every floor is a stair leading down (>), and the stairs only go down. I searched for a stair going up for longer than I'll admit. There isn't one. Whatever way out exists, it is not behind us.

  • Your torchlight reaches about seven strides. Beyond that, dark. Places you've walked stay half-remembered in your mind's eye. Trust the memory for walls, never for monsters.
  • The Well moves when you move. Stand still and it stands still with you. Waiting a beat is a legitimate tactic; I use it to make the things in the dark come to me.
  • You get one life. What the Well takes, it keeps. There's a wall on an upper floor where somebody has scratched the names of the deepest fallen. Try not to make the list.

The floors keep a rhythm. I'd stake my pack on it:

Every... The Well provides...
3rd floor (3, 6, 9, ...) A merchant, without fail - unless it's also a 10th floor, in which case the Well gives you the beast instead
10th floor (10, 20, 30, ...) One of the Crowned Ones (never a sealed door; the beast is the door)
Roughly 2 floors in 5, from the 2nd down A rune door sealing the stairs
Roughly 2 floors in 5 A chest (sometimes two, past the 10th floor)

What You Have to Work With

Whoever you were up top, down here you start the same as the rest of us did:

What At the start
Health 20
Attack 4
Defense 1
Seasoning needed to toughen 20 kills' worth, more or less

Killing teaches. When the lessons add up you get harder all at once: six more health, two more attack, one more defense, and every wound you're carrying closes on the spot. Remember that last part. It has saved my life more times than any potion: when you're bleeding out with a kill nearly in hand, finish the kill first. Each toughening comes about a third harder-won than the last.

You can carry one weapon in hand, one suit of armor on your back, and one trinket, a ring or charm that can lend either edge or hide, or a little of both.

How the Killing Goes

Walk into a thing to hit it. Simple as that, and as unforgiving.

The arithmetic, as best I've measured it across more corpses than I care to recall: your attack, less their defense, give or take a point or two of luck. Even a hopeless blow always draws at least a drop of blood. Same arithmetic applies when they hit you.

One blow in ten lands true and strikes double. Yours and theirs. Never stand in reach of something that can kill you with a lucky hit, telling yourself it probably won't get one. Probably has filled a lot of graves.

Two more things the beasts don't want you to know:

  • Most of them are asleep on their feet until they see you or you stumble into them. A dozing beast is free seasoning, or something you can simply walk around.
  • They will not climb over chests, levers, blocks, or each other. Make them come to you down a corridor and the whole pack politely queues up to die one at a time. Fighting in an open room is how you get surrounded, and surrounded is another word for dead.

The Things That Live Here

Twelve breeds, by my count, not counting the boxes with teeth (see Boxes) or the Crowned. The shallows breed vermin; the deeps breed things that were never people. Here is my ledger, with their strength as I first met them, in the shallows:

The beast Mark Health Attack Defense Worth (seasoning) Worth (gold) First appears
Rat r 5 2 0 3 2 1st floor
Giant Spider s 7 3 0 5 3 1st floor
Goblin g 9 3 1 7 5 1st floor
Kobold k 8 4 0 6 4 2nd floor
Skeleton z 12 4 2 10 6 3rd floor
Orc o 16 5 2 14 9 5th floor
Wraith w 14 7 1 16 10 7th floor
Troll T 24 8 3 24 15 9th floor
Ogre O 30 9 3 28 18 11th floor
Dark Knight K 26 10 5 32 22 14th floor
Wyvern W 34 12 4 40 28 17th floor
Lich L 30 14 3 48 35 20th floor

But mind this, because it is the single most important fact in this book: the Well feeds its children. Every floor down, everything that lives there is roughly a fifth again stronger: flesh, claw, and the worth of killing it alike. Their hides thicken a little slower. A rat on the tenth floor is three times the rat it was on the first. Do not let the name on this ledger tell you what the beast in front of you can do.

The deeper breeds crowd out the vermin as you descend; you'll mostly face whatever the Well has newest, salted with the old familiar things.

Particular warnings

  • The Giant Spider. One bite in five carries venom, and the venom does not wear off. Read The Slow Poison before you fight one, not after.
  • The Mimic. Not on the ledger above because it is not born; it hatches, out of a chest you were foolish or greedy enough to open, already awake and already angry. Kills like a beast from two floors down. The one mercy: they are stuffed with gold, being made of the stuff.
  • The woken. Fail one of the asking-doors and something nearby wakes hungry, knowing exactly where you stand. Budget for it.

The Crowned Ones

Every tenth floor, the Well goes quiet in a particular way (you'll learn the feel of it), and somewhere on that floor waits something old and crowned. Two and a half times the health of its common cousins, half again the strength, hide like a vault door.

Kill one and it's worth triple: seasoning and gold both. I've done it. I've also run, and I want you to hear this from someone with pride left to swallow: the stairs work whether or not the crowned thing is dead. It guards nothing but its own hoard. If the fight reads lethal, walk away. Triple nothing is nothing, and the scratched-name wall is full of people who did the other math.

What the Dead Leave Behind

The floors are littered with the kit of everyone who came before us. Step over a thing and it's yours; coin counts itself into your purse.

Blades and bludgeons: daggers, shortswords, longswords, axes, war hammers, rapiers, spears. The name on the steel means nothing. What matters is the sheen (see next section) and how deep it was lost; the Well seeds its floors with gear to match their dangers.

Armor: leather, chainmail, plate, studded vests, scale. Same rule.

Trinkets: rings of vigor, amulets of warding, bands of fortitude, charms of focus. Edge, hide, or a little of both.

Bottles: one swallow each, and drinking costs you a beat:

The bottle What it does
Potion of Healing Closes wounds. The deeper-found, the more it closes.
Potion of Strength Your arm is stronger forever. Drink it the moment you're safe; strength in the pack is blows never struck.
Potion of Cure Burns the poison out. Carry one always. Always.

Scrolls: one reading each, and reading costs you a beat:

The scroll What it does
Scroll of Enchantment Whets your drawn weapon permanently, or your worn armor if your hands are empty. With nothing equipped it fizzles to ash. Equip first. Learned that one the expensive way.
Scroll of Teleportation The world lurches and you are elsewhere on the floor. A door out of a losing fight. Or, rarely, a shortcut into a worse one.
Scroll of Fireball Everything your torchlight touches burns. Hoard these for the tenth floors and for the times the dark comes at you in numbers.

Keys: worth nothing to the merchants, worth everything to the lock. The Iron Key opens the locked chest hiding somewhere on its own floor; the Rune Key feeds the keyhole-door of the same name. Both key and lock always share a floor. The Well is cruel, not unfair.

On the Sheen of Things

Gear down here carries a sheen, a color in the metal that any delver learns to read at a glance. It is not decoration. It is the multiplier.

Sheen How much better How often I've seen it
Common (grey) the baseline most days
Uncommon (green) half again often enough
Rare (blue) better than twice worth a detour
Epic (violet) thrice and change a handful, ever
Legendary (gold) four and a half fold I have held three. I remember each.

The deeper you go, the more the Well tips its hand toward the fine stuff. There is no floor past which the gold sheen stops getting more likely. It's one of the reasons people keep descending. It might be the point of the whole place, for all I know.

The Merchants

Every third floor, without fail, a merchant - except where it lands on one of the Crowned Ones' floors, and there the beast takes the whole room for itself. Never once seen a merchant share a floor with one of those. Can't say I blame either of them. Every OTHER third floor: a merchant. Fat, cheerful, unbothered. And they are never short of stock, which means stock comes down from somewhere, which means they know a way in and out. I have asked. They smile. I have offered everything short of my sword arm. They smile wider. One day I'll have the gold to buy that answer, and this book will have a very different final chapter.

Until then, the practical facts:

  • Six wares at a time, a cut above what the floor gives up for free, and always, always a Potion of Cure. They know exactly what kills their customers, which tells you they're paying attention.
  • They'll buy anything you carry for half what it's worth. Coin for the walking dead, they must figure.
  • Their prices climb as you descend (call it three parts in ten dearer with every floor) and your income does not climb to match. Gold is at its mightiest in the shallows. The delver who "saves up for something legendary" is saving for a purchase that outruns them. Spend.

Boxes

Chests, the hopeful call them. They all look identical, which is precisely the problem. By my tally of every lid I've lifted:

One in... It was... And inside...
about half an honest box goods and gold, finer than floor-leavings
five trapped: a dart, or a lungful of poison the best gold of any box; danger pay, I call it: half again the coin
five or six locked the finest goods of all, once you've found the Iron Key hiding somewhere on the same floor
eight teeth your own death, hungry, with a bellyful of gold for whoever avenges you

Open every box at full health with your back to a wall, and you'll survive the arithmetic. Open one at half health because you were greedy and hurried, and... well. Wall. Names. You know the speech by now.

Some of the asking-doors leave a box behind when solved, and those are the finest boxes in the Well. More on that below.

The Floor Lies

There are stones in this place that are not stones. You cannot see them until you've stepped on them, each lies only once, and they get thicker the deeper you go; I've counted as many as six on a single deep floor.

The lie What it costs you
Spikes Blood, and more of it the deeper you are. Good armor blunts perhaps half.
Vent of gas The slow poison. See the next section, and see it now.
A rune that flares Nothing, except that you are suddenly somewhere else on the floor, and whatever was near you when you vanished is no longer your problem, and whatever is near you now is.

No cure for wariness. Walk like the floor hates you. It does.

The Slow Poison

Set this section apart in your mind, because the poison here is not like poison up top.

It does not wear off. Not in a hundred steps, not in a thousand. Only a Potion of Cure ends it. Until then it sips at you, every other step. And here is the strange mercy I've tested at the very edge: the poison itself will not land the killing drop. It leaves you at one breath from gone, clinging. It lets the rats finish you instead. I do not know why the Well arranged it so. I suspect it thinks it's funny.

You get it from the vents, from gas-rigged boxes, and from spider bites (one bite in five). A second dose doesn't stack; you simply keep the worst of the two. Small comfort.

Every merchant sells the cure. From the fourth floor onward there is no excuse to walk without one in the pack. Buying the cure after the poison means limping to the merchant at one breath from gone, praying nothing with teeth crosses the road. I've made that walk. Buy it before.

The Doors That Ask Questions

From the second floor down, some floors (two in five, by my tally) seal their stairs behind a door of graven runes. Boss floors never bother; the beast is the door. Walk into the runes and the door will put its question to you.

Three laws govern every asking-door. Carve them into your memory:

  1. Every wrong answer wakes something. Nearby, hungry, and already walking toward you. The door does not forgive; it summons.
  2. You may walk away mid-question. The door holds its state and its grudge; your progress keeps. When a door flashes something at you and demands you remember it, there is no shame in stepping back, killing whatever's drifted close, and returning composed.
  3. Solved doors sometimes pay tribute: a chest of the finest goods in the Well, left where the door stood. The simple doors almost never pay (one in seven, perhaps). The middling doors pay half the time. The cruel doors always pay. The Well rewards depth of trouble. It's the closest thing to a principle I've found down here.

The doors grow crueler as you descend. Three tiers, by my experience:

The simple doors (from the 2nd floor)

The door Its game
The Riddle of the Well An old riddle, four answers offered. The riddles repeat; I have collected every one, below.
The Odd Rune Eight runes; one is not like the others. Touch the stranger.
Counting Eyes The torchlight flares for a heartbeat over a field of vermin, then asks how many of one breed you saw. Miss, and it deals a fresh field. Count the named breed only.
The Pattern Numbers carved in a marching order; name what marches next. Differences, doublings, sums-of-the-last-two, or steps that grow by one.
The Echo Sequence The door chimes four notes and demands them back in order. A sour note buys a whole new song.

The middling doors (from the 8th floor)

The door Its game
The Number Lock Nine numbered keys; choose three that together make the carved total.
The Rune Pairs Eight tiles face down, four matched pairs beneath. Every third mismatch rings out, and wakes something.
The Nine Lanterns Touch a lantern and it flips, with its neighbors. Darken all nine inside eighteen touches, or they rekindle themselves. Corners first has served me.
The Three Levers † Three named levers scattered across the whole floor, and an inscription naming their order. One wrong pull throws them all back. Read the inscription twice. Twice.
The Pressure Plates † A row of plates by the door. Light every one with your steps. But tread a lit plate and the lot goes dark, and something wakes. Plan the walk before the first step.
The Hidden Key † A keyhole shaped like a rune, and somewhere (always the far side of the floor, in my experience) the Rune Key waiting. No trick, no penalty. Just legwork through everything that lives between.

The cruel doors (from the 16th floor)

The door Its game
The Sliding Seal The seal broken into eight sliding shards around one gap; restore their order before your allotted moves run dry, or it re-shatters.
The Rune Code Three runes of five seal the lock, in order. Seven guesses. After each, the door confesses: a + for each rune right and rightly placed, a ~ for each rune right but wandering. Waste nothing; guess to learn.
The Scales of the Deep Not a riddle but a reasoning. Guards who lie, fuses that burn unevenly, orbs and balances. My hard-won answers are below.
The Ancient Block † A stone block and a switch of rune-glass. The block moves only away from you, one shove at a time, so think three shoves ahead. Jam it in a corner and the winch by the door hauls it home. No penalty but sweat.

† These doors play out in the dungeon itself, not in the stone before you. The beasts do not pause while you work. Clear the floor first if you can.

The Answers I Have Paid For

Here is the line I promised you. Below it, every answer the asking-doors have ever accepted from me: the riddles and the reasonings both. Some were bought with a wrong guess and the fight that followed. If you want your victories over the doors to be your own, stop here and skip to Ways Out.

If you want to live, read on. I know which one I'd choose. I'm still here to write this, after all.

Ways Out, Rumored and Otherwise

You'll have noticed this book has no chapter titled "The Way Out." Here is everything I actually know, rumor honestly labeled as rumor:

The stairs only go down. Said already; bears repeating. Nobody climbs out of the Well. Whatever exit exists is below us, which is either the Well's cruelest joke or its only honest promise.

The hundredth floor. The story (and the merchants, damn their smiles, will neither confirm nor deny it) is that the Well loses its grip on those who reach the hundredth floor, and that delvers who race down fast enough have walked out alive. There are names spoken of people who did it, with the times they did it in, kept like a ledger of saints. I have never been fast enough. Perhaps you will be. Travel light, skip the fights that pay nothing, and don't stop to write books.

The numbers. Strangest of all: speak a number into the dark before your first descent, and the Well builds itself to match: every room, every beast, every box. Two delvers who whisper the same number walk the same halls, floor for floor. I don't pretend to understand it. But it means a route once found can be shared, and to a delver racing for the hundredth floor, a proven road is worth more than gold.

The Well remembers. Every step ever taken in it, they say, and some delvers have learned to read a departed traveler's whole descent back like a story, from first step to last. I find it comforting, in a grim way. Somewhere in the Well's long memory, every name on the wall is still walking.

What Has Kept Me Alive

Twelve rules. Each one has a story; most of the stories have a body in them. In the order I'd tattoo them on a new delver:

  1. Fight in doorways and corridors. One set of teeth at a time. An open room is an invitation to be surrounded, and I've watched surrounded happen to better fighters than either of us.
  2. Equip before you enchant. The scroll's magic needs somewhere to go. Bare hands turn it to ash.
  3. Carry the cure from the fourth floor on. The poison is patient and permanent, and the walk to a merchant at one breath from gone is the longest walk there is.
  4. A dozing beast is your choice to make. Seasoning if you want it, scenery if you don't. Never wake what you haven't priced.
  5. Wounds don't close on their own. Bottles close them, and toughening closes them. So when you're bleeding with a kill in reach, kill first, drink after. The toughening heals everything, and the potion keeps.
  6. Open boxes at full health. One in eight has teeth and one in five has a sting, and the box does not care how unlucky you've already been today.
  7. Respect the asking-doors' price. Every miss is a monster. For the flashing, chiming, remembering doors: step away, clear the room, come back calm. The door keeps your progress. Your nerves are the only thing that resets.
  8. The keyhole door and the block door cannot hurt you, only tire you. The levers and the plates can. Know which kind you're facing before you touch anything.
  9. Hoard fire for crowds and crowns. A fireball scroll spent on one rat is a story you'll be ashamed to tell.
  10. Spend gold young. Prices deepen faster than purses. The blade you can afford today kills the beasts standing between you and a better one.
  11. The crowned owe you nothing and the stairs are always open. Fight for triple or walk for free, but decide with your head, not your pride.
  12. Strength drunk is strength owned. A bottle of it riding in your pack is every blow it would have hardened, wasted.

Appendix: Controls

(The one page of this book its author didn't write.)

Key Action
Arrows / WASD Move / attack (bump into monsters)
E Inventory
. or Z Wait a turn
M Toggle sound
Enter / double-click Use, equip, buy or sell the selected item
Tab Switch between Buy and Sell in shops
D Drop the selected inventory item
Esc Close menu

On phones and tablets: an on-screen D-pad (hold a direction to keep walking, the center dot waits a turn), plus Bag / Sound / Fullscreen buttons. Tap an item for its stats; double-tap or use the action button to use, equip, buy or sell.

If you've read this far: take the pack I've buried nothing in, because I trust no floor to stay where I left it. Take instead everything above, which is the only treasure I've found down here that weighs nothing.

Descend fast. Spend young. Count everything.

And if you find the door out, wedge it open. Some of us are still down here.

Unsigned. Still descending.
